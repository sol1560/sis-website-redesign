Where it began

1996

One Singaporean educator. One school in Jakarta. One belief.

In 1996, Singaporean educator Jaspal Sidhu opened the first Singapore Intercultural School in Jakarta. He built it on Singapore's education model and on a simple conviction: a world-class education should not be out of reach for the families who want it most.

Three decades later, that conviction runs through more than seventeen campuses in four countries, and it still shapes every classroom.

“Quality education that is trusted, consistent, and accessible across communities and generations.” The SIS commitment

    Desired student learning outcomes

    Every child learns at their own pace. All of them learn PACE.

    PACE sets out the 21st-century skills that SIS builds into the curriculum at every campus. Here are the four.

    Now reading: Perseverance

    01 · P

    Perseverance

    Students set goals, work steadily toward them, and treat feedback as a tool. When something doesn't work, they revise their approach and keep going.

    Questions to ask: “What will I try next?” and “What did the feedback tell me?”

    02 · A

    Analytical Thinking

    Students weigh different perspectives, form hypotheses and test them against evidence. They learn to reason through a problem instead of guessing at it.

    Questions to ask: “How do I know?” and “What would change my mind?”

    03 · C

    Communication & Collaboration

    In schools where many languages meet, students learn to express ideas clearly in more than one language and to contribute to a team as both leader and listener.

    Questions to ask: “Did everyone understand?” and “What did my team need from me?”

    04 · E

    Entrepreneurship & Innovation

    Using design thinking, students spot real problems, prototype solutions and test whether they work. Ideas become useful when someone builds them.

    Questions to ask: “Who is this for?” and “How could it work better?”

    Academics

    One continuous path, from first steps to university.

    SIS students can stay on one connected pathway from preschool through to Junior College, taught with a consistent approach on every campus.

    1. 1

      Preschool

      Play-based discovery, first friendships, and early literacy and numeracy.

    2. 2

      Primary

      Strong foundations in English, mathematics and science, taught in the Singapore tradition.

    3. 3

      Secondary

      Broader subjects, more independent inquiry, and international qualifications.

    4. 4

      Junior College

      Pre-university study with university counselling.
